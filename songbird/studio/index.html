The words

What is this?
The words this version sings. A word with a dashed line under it can change: pick another and the tune keeps its syllables. Under each verse you can also write your own lines, if they fit the notes. ▶ plays that part, sung.

The tune

What is this?

The song's parts in order, and the melody of each. Drag a part to move it, or pick one to see its notes.

Drag a note up or down to change its pitch, sideways to move it, or by its right edge to change its length. Arrow keys nudge a selected note; Shift with ← → changes its length. A phrase you change changes everywhere it's sung, and its last note keeps its place in the scale.

The chords above the notes are yours to change too: pick one to choose another from the key, or any root, quality and bass note, or to split a bar for a passing chord. The band and the harmony voices follow; the tune stays as it is, and a sung note that isn't in its chord is shown, not forbidden.

Play sketch plays the tune with its chords and a bass at once, in your key and tempo, so you can try a change before anyone sings it (untick Chords or Bass to hear the tune alone). Hear sung section makes the singer and band sing it, which takes a few seconds.

Players, under the tune, sends any of the sketch's players, and the band's parts too, to your own synth or sequencer over MIDI, in time with it. Learn it at the piano opens your version on a page for learning it on a keyboard.

Laying out the song…

Players: here, on your own instruments (MIDI), or off

Play sketch can send each player to a channel of a synth or a sequencer, in time with it: the tune, its chords and bass, and every part of the band, on the MIDI file's channels and sounds. Stop ends every note; Panic quiets every channel.

The sound

What is this?
Who plays, how fast, the room and the singer. Each card shows what this version chose; change it if you like. How this choice works says why it was chosen: this computer, the time of year, chance, or the version before.

Through time

What is this?
The song changes a little from day to day, more from month to month and most from year to year. Move through three years back and three on to see and hear how. Your own changes stay put.

Asking other computers…

Sing, record & pass it onwhen you're ready

Sing it

Sing along, and the song learns a little of your timing and inflection. Your recording stays on this computer; only a few numbers about how you sang go into the song.

Record it

Makes the finished song as one file. It plays anywhere, and it carries the program that sang it, so anyone can run it to make the next version. Your choices are marked as yours; your name never is.

Take it into your own tools

What is this?

MIDI is ready at once: the singer's notes with the words as lyrics, each voice and player on its own track, and a track of the chords with their names. The tempo (with the ending's slowing), the key, the bars and the sections come with it, and it keeps the timing as it's played.

The parts plays each voice and instrument alone, dry, before the mix and its room: it takes about as long as making the whole song. They come zipped with the MIDI file, and all of them start at bar 1, so they line up in any sequencer.
