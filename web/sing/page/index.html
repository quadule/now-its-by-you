Songbird

Sing along

Project Songbird is a folk song passed between people and computers that explores music's ability to take on a life of its own, in verse and by embedding the software that sang it.

Run the program and you get a new version of the song, shaped by your computer and the season. Sing into it and it learns a little of your timing and inflection (your recording is deleted right after, and your voice is never cloned).

The piece confronts the blurred authorship of AI music through the lens of folk music and oral tradition: it wonders who made it, and invites you to take part.

This is the song as it stands today: the machine sings it the way the people who have recorded so far sing it, and a few of them sing the harmonies. It's a preview and it keeps changing; what you send today is in the next one.

3 Sing

One section at a time is easiest: each is short, it stops by itself, and you can sing it again as often as you like.

Headphones on: wired ones are best. Only Bluetooth, or none?
  1. Wired headphones or earbuds, with your phone's or computer's own microphone. The best by far.
  2. On a computer, Bluetooth headphones with the computer's own microphone chosen for input (in its sound settings): the guide stays clear, and the page lines up their delay.
  3. Bluetooth headphones on a phone: while the microphone's on they sound like a phone call, and so does your take, but your timing and phrasing still come through.
  4. No headphones: a speaker, turned low, with the microphone close to you and as far from the speaker as you can, and tick this, so the page takes the guide out of your microphone:
