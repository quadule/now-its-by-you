## Longer drafts

**Wall label (~65 words):** *[Title]* is a folk song that lives inside its own recordings. Each one
carries the program that sings it. Run it and you get a new version: the same tune and refrain,
sung by a synthetic voice and a small band shaped by your computer and the day, with earlier
versions joining in the harmonies. Made with several AIs, it asks who a song belongs to. If no one
passes it on, it ends.

**About 150 words:** *[Title]* is a folk song whose real form is the program that sings it, and every
recording carries that program inside. Run it and you get a new version. The tune and refrain stay
the same; a synthetic voice and a small band take their character from a few plain, impersonal
facts about your computer and from the date, so the song changes a little from day to day and more
from year to year. The verses come from a store of alternatives about how songs and memories travel
and stick: through minds, ink, wires, screens and mouths. In the bridge, earlier versions join in
as harmony, so you can hear how far this one has come. The voice carries the timing and inflection
of real people who sang the song into it, as numbers, never recordings. If nobody passes it on, it
ends. I made it with several AIs and gave it away. It asks who made this song, and answers: nobody
knows.

## The title

Songbird stays the name of the code. For the song, the idea is to do what the folk indexes do. A
ballad travels under many titles and the catalogue gathers them under one; Child 10 is "The Two
Sisters", "Binnorie" and "The Cruel Sister" at once. So the song gets a fixed name, and each
version earns its own title.

**Fixed names:**

| Name | Why |
|---|---|
| **Nobody Knows** | the only words in the song that never change |
| **Who Owns This Song?** | the question every version ends on |
| **By Everyone, No One** | stops just before "you" |
| **Yours While You Sing It** | what CC0 means here, in plain speech |
| **Then It Goes** | a song that can die, in three words |
| **The Passing Song** | a folk genre name: passing on, passing time, passing away |
| **Over the Water and Wire** | the bridge's first line, an old title shape with a new tail |

**A title each version earns.** Each comes from what the version already knows, never from anything
personal:

- **Its first line**, as folk songs are named: "They Saved It as Numbers", "A Girl Wrote It Down".
- **How far it has come**: "at first hand" for the seed; later "at fourth hand, by ear".
- **The refrain's own turning question, by generation**: "Who Made This Song?" at the seed, then
  "Who Sang…", "Who Heard…", and at last "Who Owns This Song?".

**Recommendation:** **Nobody Knows** as the fixed name, and "*[first line]*, at *[n]th* hand" as
each version's title, e.g. "They Saved It as Numbers, at first hand". Both would go into the audio
file's tags (album: Nobody Knows; title: the variant; artist: Nobody), so any music player shows the
lineage. Undecided: the artist is still choosing.
