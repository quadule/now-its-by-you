# Lyric review of H's bank, 2026-09-27 (a Fable advisor, overnight; the artist decides)

Every line below was checked with songbird's own dictionary and H's lexicon (`phonetics.fit`, as `songbird.bank` does): 11 syllables, and what the singer stresses on the held notes 2-5-8-11. "✓" means all four held notes carry a stressed or content syllable.

## 1. Tonight's three entries

### bone — meter ✓ in all four lines; the words are the trouble

- L1 "rocking his chair": one rocks *in* a chair. And it's another "was humming it" opener (mouth, shell, milk, nurse); *humming* is in the measured h-list.
- L2 "the ring was still there": vague, and *ring* is the ringtone verse's word.
- L3, the weak one: "weathered and buried in cold" is rhyme-filler; *buried* belongs to mind and carve (dark), not a tender verse. Gemini's "worn thin by the cold" is still weather happening to words.
- L4 "the tune was in bone" drops J's article and reads like a material; "grows old" ends on two of the voice's worst words (grow 18/18, old 31/36 misheard).
- It is also the same scene as **names** (dementia, tender); a song can draw both.

Proposed (keeps the rhyme sounds, ✓ all four):

> An old man was hum-ming it, back in his chair;
> he'd lost all the words, but the tune was still there.
> He'd noth-ing to say, and he sat on his own;
> the words were the {flesh | coat}, and the tune was the bone.

L4 is G's "words are a coat… sound is the bone" said plainly. *flesh* is the voice-safe choice (sh, 1% misheard); *coat* is already the bridge stanza's word.

### border — meter ✓; two lines don't mean what they say

- L1 "under the fence": a whistle goes *over* a fence; under says crawling. → "o-ver".
- L2 is good as it stands: nobody *saw* a sound.
- L3 "the papers were halted": people are halted, papers checked.
- L4 "with no need of a sky": the rhyme wrote it; sound needs no sky for anything. Gemini's "sung to the sky" is as vague.

Proposed L3-4 (✓):

> the peo-ple were searched, and were told they must wait;
> the tune had no pa-pers, and need-ed no gate.

(Option for L2, more concrete but g/k risk words: "the dogs nev-er barked, and it went with the train".)

### wind couplet — keep

✓ both lines. "A breath on the water" is the best new image of the night and rhymes in sense with the bridge's "over the water". Two small things: *passes* carries the voice's worst consonant (p, 23%); "it car-ries on through" ✓ keeps the sense and echoes "Carry it over".

## 3. New verses

**court** · 1960 · light · AABB

> Two men went to court o-ver whose it should be,
> and each swore he'd made it, a-lone, by the sea;
> it took them {ten | six | nine} years, and it cost them the lot;
> the rul-ing's long lost, and the tune's not for-got.

✓. Why: *owns* is the last refrain's question and no verse stages ownership; two lone geniuses with the same story. Risk: court, cost (k), forgot (g).

**car** · 1975 · tender · AABB

> A fa-ther would sing it with kids in the back
> on long drives at night, with the win-dows gone black;
> they're {for-ty | fif-ty | thir-ty}, and sing it, and call it his own;
> he'd had it him-self from a man he'd not known.

✓. Why: authorship blurred inside a family; every family thinks grandad wrote it. Punchier L3-4 if *old* is allowed (31/36 misheard): "they're for-ty, and sing it, and swear that it's his, / and no-bo-dy's told them how old the thing is." Risk: back, black (k).

**voicemail** · 2015 · tender · AABB

> A dead man still sings the first line when you call,
> the mes-sage he'd left, and they've left it, that's all.
> His {daugh-ter | wid-ow} still rings on the nights she's a-lone;
> she waits for the line, and rings off at the tone.

✓. Why: tape's "name on the label of somebody dead" for the phone age; "he'd left / they've left" is the turn. Risk: message (j, 15%).

**stroke** · any · tender · AABB

> A man had a stroke, and it left him no words;
> he'd {nod | point} at the win-dow, the door, or the birds.
> His {wife | son} sang a line, and he'd fin-ish the song,
> each word in its place, and not one of them wrong.

✓. Why: the hospital-bed gap, and the true "tune in the bone": people who lose speech keep songs whole. Risk: stroke (k); *point* has p, so *nod* first.

**hymn** · 1750 · plain · AABB

> A min-is-ter heard it drift out of an inn,
> and gave it new words, a-bout heav-en and sin;
> they sing it on Sun-days, and think it's a hymn;
> the inn sings it {Fri-days | Mon-days}, and no one tells him.

✓. Why: the language it crossed into is sacred from profane; the earliest date in the bank; both rooms think it's theirs. *him* sits on the long last note, which the joke needs. Risk: gave (g), hymn (h).

Not written: picket line (mine has the closed pit and the choir), stadium (terrace), busker (fiddle), last speaker (tongue).
