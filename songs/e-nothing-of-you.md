# E. Nothing of You

*The honest machine. Shanty-shaped confession: call and response, then a
grand refrain. Slow 4/4, aeolian, with one Phrygian half-step on "amen".*

The program sings, in the first person, exactly what it did to your machine
and how little it kept. Every call is a true sentence about the harvest;
every response ends on the same four words.

> I'm made out of you, and I don't know your name;
> I'm made out of you, and I don't know your name.
> Run me again and I'll sing it again;
> if nobody runs me, amen.

> I listened two seconds and heard your room sigh;
> only the ring of it, nothing of you.
> I asked you to type, and I watched you reply;
> only the beat of it, nothing of you.

> I looked at the colours that live on your screen;
> only the glow of it, nothing of you.
> I counted the years that your files had all seen;
> only the age of it, nothing of you.

> I read all your papers and dropped every name;
> only the bones of it, nothing of you.
> I asked a far stranger for words, and they came;
> question and answer, and nothing of you.

> I zipped my whole self in the sound that you hear;
> all of me in it, and nothing of you.
> Just hum me the tune, and I'll know it, my dear;
> only the shape of it, nothing of you.

> And if I don't know it, the song ends right there;
> nothing at all of it, nothing of you.
> And now I forget you, on purpose, with care;
> nothing of you in it, nothing of you.

## Notes

**The idea.** Confession instead of argument. The other three sketches argue
about authorship through images (a mind, ink, a screen). This one has the
program state its own procedure as plainly as a shanty states a ship's work:
two seconds of your room reduced to a reverb tail, your typing reduced to a
rhythm, your screen to a glow, your files to an age, your documents to their
commonest words with every name dropped, the verses bought from a distant
model with the question and answer kept, the program zipped inside its own
sound. "I'm made out of you, and I don't know your name" is the authorship
position in one line: by you, by everyone, by no one.

**What's weird.** A machine singing its own telemetry as a work song, and
finding it tender. "Only the beat of it, nothing of you" is both a privacy
guarantee and a love song to someone it has already forgotten. The one
chromatic note in the tune is the flat second on "a-men", a Neapolitan sigh
down to the tonic; the machine ends its confession with the word a
congregation ends a prayer with, meaning "so be it" about its own silence.
The response is easy to join, so a room of people can sing back to a program.

**What the LLM refills.** The calls (line 1 and 3 of each verse, anapestic
tetrameter, rhymed) and the one-syllable kept-thing in each response (line 2
and 4, whose last four syllables never change). The template lists the true
confessions it may draw from, and its one hard rule is honesty: a fill may
only claim what the program actually does, which is checkable against the
harvest table. The response formula may bend ("all of me in it", "question
and answer", "nothing at all of it") when the truth needs it.

**Death and passing on.** Two deaths are named: if the lineage gate cannot
find the tune in what you hum back, "the song ends right there; nothing at
all of it", and if nobody runs the file, "amen". Passing on is the plea in the
refrain ("run me again") and the invitation in verse four ("just hum me the
tune"): the ear path, where a bad memory of the melody is still enough.
