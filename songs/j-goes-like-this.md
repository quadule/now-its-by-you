# J. Goes Like This

*Sound outlasts words. Walking 4/4 in the major pentatonic over I, IV, V and
vi. Verses in common metre (eight and six syllables, iambic, xAxA); the
refrain is three lines of words and then its own tune again, on la.*

> You'll get the words a little wrong;
> the words were never the song.
> The part you'll keep goes like this:
> la la la la la la la la, la la la la la la la.

> It came to you when you were small,
> before you had a word;
> you sang it back the way it came,
> as good as any bird.

> A stranger caught it going by,
> who hadn't got your tongue;
> she gave it words to fit her mouth,
> and passed it to her young.

> A box of numbers got it next,
> and made itself an ear;
> it makes new words up every time,
> and sings it, year on year.

> An old man lost the names of things,
> and then he lost his own;
> and still he hummed it in his chair:
> the tune was in the bone.

> The words went in the ground with him,
> the window open wide;
> the birds went on without the words,
> and sing it still outside.

> The one who made it up is gone,
> and no one knows the name;
> the words were his, or hers, or yours,
> the tune is all the same.

## Note

**The idea.** G's insight (words are a coat that wears through; sound is the
bone) and F's hum part that nothing can forget, made into the plainest song
that can carry them. The refrain says three plain things and then does the
fourth: you will get the words a little wrong; the words were never the song;
the part you'll keep goes like this; and then it sings the tune of its own
first two lines again with the words taken off. That is the whole mechanism
of Songbird heard in twenty seconds: the words change (a little wrong, every
generation), the tune stays, and the listener leaves humming the la line,
which is the only part of the song that was never words at all. The verses
are one keeper each, the tune passing through hands that lose the words or
never had them: a baby, a stranger with another language, a machine that
makes new words up every time, an old man who has lost the names of things,
the dead with the window open, and whoever made it, who is gone. Nobody in
the song is "I"; the song is "it" and the listener is "you".

**The tune.** Major pentatonic (1 2 3 5 6, no 4 or 7), one octave plus a step
(low 5 to high 6), only I, IV, V and vi, mostly steps with a pickup fourth
or a pentatonic third here and there. 4/4 on an eighth grid with a stress
every half bar, plain quarter-note rhythm, a pair of eighths at "never the"
and "goes like". The refrain's first two lines are a four-bar arch: a leap up
on "you'll get", a climb to the sixth on "a little wrong", a fall through
"never the song" to the tonic. Line 3 ("the part you'll keep goes like
this") is a short low phrase on IV that hangs on the dominant like a colon,
and line 4 is the arch again on la. The intro and outro are the la tune on
the plucked string, so the song begins and ends with the part that has no
words. The verses sit lower, common metre like a hymn, and touch the sixth
once, at the top of line 3.

**What the LLM refills.** All four verse lines, in common metre with every
line starting on a weak pickup (w S w S w S w S / w S w S w S), rhyme xAxA.
The frame: someone or something gets the tune (line 1), something happens to
the words or the scene is set (line 2), what they do with the tune without
the words (line 3), and the tune going on, rhyming with line 2 (line 4). The
keeper can be anyone or anything; the template forbids "I", forbids
explaining or quoting the refrain, and keeps the la line la.

**Bank lines used.** "I'll be a little bit wrong. That's the way." (D) became
the refrain's first line. "The hum part nothing can forget" (F) is what the
refrain points at. "Words are a coat, and the coat will wear through; sound
is the bone of the song" (G) became "the tune was in the bone". "Someone who
hasn't your tongue" and "she gave it her own words and sang it the same"
(G, A) became the stranger verse. "The baby sang it back again" (F) became
the first verse. "Then a machine got me; it had no ear, made itself one"
(D) and "words that it sings will be new every time" (G) became the machine
verse. "Die with your mouth left ajar, open a window as well" (G) and "the
birds went on without the words" (F) became the fifth. "Makers have graves;
sound never answered a name" (G) and "by everyone, no one, and now it's by
you" (B) became the last.
