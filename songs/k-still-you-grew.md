# K. Still You Grew

*The song as a named character. Daisy is a lawn daisy: nobody plants her,
everyone picks her, and she comes up again in any grass, uninvited. Bright 4/4
in D major pentatonic (do re mi so la only), chords I, IV, V and vi, range low
so to la. Verses in trochaic sevens (DUM da DUM da DUM da DUM), AABB, with a
long-short skip; the refrain is two questions with one answer, twice.*

Her life in six verses, told in one family of images and never saying "song":
how she came up where nobody sowed her, how she lived in people's hands, how
they tried to be rid of her or forgot her, and how a stranger's child, years
from now or long ago, finds her and does with her what the first children did.
The refrain asks her the two questions the verses keep answering.

> Oh Daisy, who planted you?
> Nobody did, and still you grew.
> Oh Daisy, who wanted you?
> Nobody did, and still you grew.

> No one sowed her, no one knew;
> half was wind and half was shoe.
> Up she came between two stones,
> no one's garden, all her own.

> Someone pulled her petals: yes,
> no, and yes, and no, and yes.
> All she meant, she gave away;
> still the yellow eye would stay.

> Children split her stems and strung
> every one of her on one;
> round their necks and through their hair,
> off she went to who knows where.

> Something blind came roaring past,
> cut the whole world down to grass;
> low she learned to flower, low;
> blind, it passed, and didn't know.

> Those who wore her, old and grey,
> lost her name and went away;
> where they laid them, row on row,
> she's the only thing they grow.

> Someone's child, in someone's grass,
> years from now, or long since past,
> split her stem, the way you do,
> pulled another daisy through.

**Template:** one chapter of Daisy's life per verse, in order (came up unsown /
lived in hands / they tried to be rid of her, or forgot her / a stranger's
child finds her and threads her again). Four lines of seven syllables, DUM da
DUM da DUM da DUM, AABB. Third person: Daisy is "she", every daisy is one of
her, "you" is the listener and appears only in the last verse, nobody is "I".
Lawn images only: never song, tune, sing, hum, music, machine, computer or
program; no paper, books, ink or pages; nothing gross.

## Notes

- **The idea.** The artist's "Oh Melody" approach: the song is a character
  with a name and a life, told in one family of images, and a stranger ends
  it by retracing the shape that made her without knowing. Daisy carries the
  new heart of the project, stickiness: nobody plants her and nobody asks for
  her, she comes up in every lawn anyway, the mower and the frost only make
  her flower lower, and she is half meaningless (a fortune told in petals,
  "all she meant, she gave away") while the yellow eye stays. Passing-on is in
  the background: the children's chains, "every one of her on one", are the
  lineage, and the last verse is a stranger's child making the same chain.
  The dead who forgot her name are "the only thing they grow": pushing up
  daisies, the gentlest way to say the song outlives its keepers.
- **The name.** Plain, two syllables, easy for a formant voice (D, a long
  diphthong, Z, a long vowel). It carries its meaning sideways: "day's eye",
  the flower that closes at night and opens again every morning; "pushing up
  daisies"; "fresh as a daisy"; the daisy chain; and, for anyone who knows,
  "Daisy Bell" was the first song a computer ever sang (an IBM 7094 in 1961),
  and the one HAL sings as he is switched off. The lyric never quotes it.
- **The refrain.** Two questions with one answer, and the answer is the hook:
  "Nobody did, and still you grew" twice. "Planted" and "wanted" chime, which
  makes the refrain a little nursery-rhyme-like on purpose. The "you" of the
  refrain is Daisy; the "you" of the last verse is the listener, and the
  overlap is meant. "Who wanted you?" is the earworm question: it sticks
  whether you want it or not. Nothing in the refrain pins down what the
  program reads or does.
- **The tune.** Major pentatonic, no 4 or 7, over I, IV, V and vi, at 112.
  The refrain is a high call and a low answer: "Oh Daisy" leaps mi to la and
  rocks la-so-mi-so-la-so, ending open on so over V with a quarter's breath;
  "Nobody did, and still you grew" answers from do, do-do-re-mi-re, and closes
  mi-re-do over vi-I the first time and so-mi-do over V-I the second. The
  verses sit low (low so to la) in a long-short skip, ♩. ♪ ♩. ♪ ♩. ♪ 𝅗𝅥, the
  rhythm of a trochee spoken aloud: line 1 do-re-mi then down to low la (vi),
  line 2 up to so and half-closing on re, line 3 the lift on so and la, line 4
  falling so-mi-re-do and bouncing la-so-do home. Everything is a step or a
  pentatonic third except the fourth onto "Daisy". Plain by design: the drift
  has room to move mode, harmony and colour.
- **What the LLM refills.** All four verse lines, from the template above, one
  chapter per verse in order. Every generation keeps a verse in which nobody
  sowed her and still she came, a verse in which she means nothing and is
  asked anyway, something blind that cuts the grass and never knows she is
  there, and a last verse in which a stranger's child threads another daisy
  through her. The refrain never changes.
- **Bank lines used.** "It's nobody's making and everyone's own" (B) is behind
  "no one's garden, all her own"; "by everyone, no one" (B) and "Who made this
  song? Nobody knows" (A) are behind "Nobody did"; "she gave it her own words"
  (A) and "the words were never the song" (J) are behind the petal verse;
  "made itself an ear" (D, J) is answered by "something blind... didn't know";
  "the tune was in the bone" (J) and "they buried it with him" (A) are behind
  "the only thing they grow". The daisy, the wind and the shoe, the chain and
  the yellow eye are new.
