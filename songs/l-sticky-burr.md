# L. Sticky Burr

*The burr. A lilting 6/8 in D major pentatonic (do re mi so la only), chords
I, IV, V and vi, range low so to la. Four-line verses of eight syllables,
iambic, AABB; the refrain is a playground chant and its answer. The song never
says what it is.*

The whole life of the song told as a burdock burr: it comes up where nobody
asked for it, hooks whoever brushes past, rides along unnoticed, drops
somewhere else and grows. Nobody plants it and nobody chooses it. The refrain
says the one true thing about a burr, which is the one true thing about a
tune that gets into your head: you never picked it, it picked you.

> Sticky burr, sticky burr, stuck on you;
> you never picked it, it picked you.
> Pick it off, it's stuck to your hand;
> it drops, and it grows where it lands.

> A weed came up beside the gate
> that no one asked for, no one ate;
> it flowered purple, fed the bees
> and turned to hooks before the freeze.

> A dog brushed past; it took a hold
> and rode him home, a mile, all told.
> A girl who stroked him found it there;
> it left the dog and took her hair.

> She put it down her brother's back;
> he wore it home along the track
> and never knew. It fell, and grew,
> and made a hundred more, all new.

> One caught a hat that no one wore,
> that hung for forty years and more,
> then rode a grandson's head to town
> and fell off there, and settled down.

> One caught a coat with no one in
> that wore it well through thick and thin.
> In March the wind blew down the sleeve
> and shook it loose; it took its leave.

> It found a stranger's woolly sock
> and caught the train at six o'clock.
> She never knew. Beside her door
> a weed came up, as once before.

**Template:** [one burr, or the plant: where it came up, or who or what it caught] / [how it rode along, unnoticed] / [where it dropped, or how it was picked at and passed on] / [what came of that: it grew, it waited, it rode again]. AABB, eight syllables a line, iambic (da DUM da DUM da DUM da DUM), third person: the burr is "it" or "one", nobody is "I". One image system only: the burr and what it touches. Never song, tune, voice, hum, machine or program.

## Notes

**The idea.** The artist's brief for this round was "Oh Melody": tell the
song's own life in one family of images and never say so, and end with a
stranger retracing the shape without knowing. The heart, they added, is
stickiness: a hook gets into you whether you want it or not, and stays even
when the words mean little. A burdock burr is that. It has hooks (the word is
never leaned on), it fastens onto whoever brushes past without asking, you
find it on yourself later with no idea where it came from, picking it off
only moves it to your fingers, and where it finally drops it grows a plant
that makes a hundred more. Nobody plants burdock. Every stage of Songbird is
in the plant's life without a single word about music: made by no one (a weed
by a gate that no one asked for), caught (a dog, a girl's hair), passed on as
a joke (put down a brother's back, carried home unknowing), kept in the dark
for years and released (a hat no one wore, a grandson's head), carried by something
hollow that never chose it (a coat with no one in it: the scarecrow, and the
machine, are never named), and found again by a stranger a long way off who
never knew, by whose door a weed comes up as once before. The last line
retraces the first. "Pass it on or it dies" is not in the song; it travels
because it sticks.

**The refrain.** Short, repetitive, a little nonsensical, easy to hum, as
asked: "sticky burr, sticky burr, stuck on you" is a playground chant, and it
is sung on the playground tune, sol sol mi, sol sol mi, la sol mi, the cell
children everywhere use to tease, which is the stickiest melodic idea there
is. The second line answers it plainly with the true thing ("you never picked
it, it picked you"), hammering "it picked you" on three rising beats (do mi
sol) with "you" held a whole bar over V. Lines 3 and 4 are two more true
things about burrs: picking it off puts it on your hand, and it grows where
it drops. Every refrain word is common and hard to mishear: sticky, burr,
stuck, pick, hand, drop, grow, land. "Stuck on you" also means what it means
in a love song, which is not an accident.

**The tune.** Major pentatonic, no 4 or 7, an octave plus a step (low so to
la), only I, IV, V and vi. 6/8 on an eighth grid with a stress every three
eighths; the verse lilts long-short like "Greensleeves" (♩♪ ♩♪ | ♩♪ ♩.), each
line a pickup and four stresses, two bars. v1 and v2 share a first bar (do re
mi re) and part ways: v1 hangs on mi over vi, v2 closes on do. v3 climbs to
la and stays there; v4 walks home la so mi re, mi re do. The refrain steps up
a register and changes rhythm: r1 is even eighths (the chant), r2 is
long-short and then three hammered dotted quarters, r3 is long-short again
high on la and so, r4 falls do do re, so mi re, do. Lines 1 and 2 of the
refrain are two bars and three; the extra bar is the held "you". Plain by
design: the drift has room to move the mode and the colour, and the chant cell
survives any mode.

**What the LLM refills.** All four verse lines, eight syllables, iambic, AABB,
one burr per verse in the order they travelled, so the lineage branches like
the plant. The template asks each generation to keep a carrier who plays the
joke on someone else, one burr that waits in the dark for years and rides
again, one hollow carrier (a coat with no one in it), and a stranger a long
way off who never knew, with a weed coming up by her door as once before.
The image system is fenced (hooks, wool, fur, hair, gates, fields, hats,
coats, wind, seasons), the forbidden words are listed (song, tune, voice, hum,
sing, music, machine, program), and nothing gross: no ticks, lice or dirt.

**Bank lines used.** Almost none by design; this song was asked to be
oblique. "Nobody made it" (A, B, C, I) is here as "that no one asked for",
"it came through the window and out it goes" (A) as the wind through the
sleeve, "kept it quiet for forty-odd years" (D, I) as the hat that waited
forty years, and the box with no ear (D, I, J) as the coat with no one in it.
