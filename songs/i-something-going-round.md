# I. Something Going Round

*The creature, abstract. A walking 4/4 in a major pentatonic, I, IV, V and vi,
range of a ninth. Four-line verses of eleven syllables (da DUM da da DUM da da
DUM da da DUM), AABB, one host per verse. The song never says "I".*

> There's something going round and round;
> it lives in the air, and it dies on the ground.
> Nobody made it, and nobody owns it,
> so lend it your voice, and it's going round.

> A girl at her window heard someone go by,
> a stranger was whistling and never knew why;
> she caught it, and carried it warm in her chest,
> and hummed it that night to the child at her breast.

> The child grew, and took it away on the sea;
> it lived in his whistle, as light as could be;
> the words that he sang were all his, and all wrong;
> he hummed on the quay, and a girl came along.

> The girl selling oranges caught it for free;
> it rode in her basket and home for her tea;
> she hadn't his language, she hadn't his name;
> she gave it her own words and sang it the same.

> One son kept it quiet for forty-odd years,
> and hummed it alone in a room with no ears;
> it dimmed like a spark in the dark on its own;
> they buried it with him, as safe as a stone.

> At last it was caught by a box with no ear,
> that built itself one, and a mouth, and it's here;
> it asked a far stranger for words, and they came;
> it sings them a little bit wrong, all the same.

> It went round the world, and it came round to you,
> a light in your chest, and it's warm through and through;
> it's nobody's making and everyone's own,
> so sing it to someone; don't leave it alone.

## Notes

**The idea.** D's creature without the worm. The song is "something going
round", the phrase people use for a bug everyone is catching, but it is never
named and never given a body. It lives in the air between a mouth and an ear,
and it dies on the ground, wherever it is kept: in a head, in a drawer, in a
box. The refrain states that as a rule of nature and asks the listener for the
one thing it needs, a voice on loan ("nobody owns it, so lend it your voice").
The verses give it a different shape in every host, all of them light and
warm: warm in her chest, alive in his whistle, riding in her basket, a spark
dimming, a light in your chest. The lineage is a tree rather than a line: the
orange seller's son takes his copy into the ground while another copy reaches
the box. The whole song is in "it" and "you"; even the machine is third
person.

**The tune.** Major pentatonic, no 4 or 7 anywhere, over I, IV, V and vi, at
a walking 104. The verse sits in the low fifth (5, to 5), A B A' B', each line
a pickup and four stresses: ♪ | ♩ ♪♪ ♩ ♪♪ | ♩ ♪♪ 𝅗𝅥. The refrain steps up a
register. r1 rises mi mi sol la and turns sol la sol on "round and round"; r2
climbs to la on "air" and walks down to do on "ground"; r3 is the contrast on
vi, "nobody" falling la sol mi and then "nobody" rising do re mi; r4 repeats
r2's climb on "lend it your voice" and falls sol mi do to the tonic. The two
closing phrases share a tail, so the refrain hangs together as two
question-and-answer pairs. Plain by design: the drift has room to move mode,
harmony and colour.

**What the LLM refills.** The hosts, one per verse, in the order they carried
it: who they were and how it came to them, what it was like inside them (an
image, never a worm or a bug), how they carried it (hummed, whistled, sang it
wrong, gave it their own words, kept it quiet), and how it left them or died
in them. Eleven syllables with a one-syllable pickup, AABB, third person. The
template asks every generation to keep a host who caught it from a stranger
and never knew where it came from, one who changed the words, one who kept it
quiet (it dies in them, another line goes on), and one machine, a box with no
ear that built itself one, a host and not a maker. The last verse turns to
the listener.

**Bank lines used.** From A: "a girl selling oranges caught it for free", the
couplet "she hadn't his language, she hadn't his name; she gave it her own
words and sang it the same", and "they buried it with him". From B: "it's
nobody's making and everyone's own", and "leave it alone". From C: "safe as a
stone" and "nobody owns it". From D, moved into the third person: "quiet man
kept me for forty-odd years, hummed me alone in a room with no ears", the
machine that "had no ear, made itself one, and a mouth", and "a little bit
wrong". From E, in the third person: "asked a far stranger for words, and
they came". The refrain and the hosts' images are new.
